Is it safe to paste customer data into ChatGPT?

Short answer: not by default. Rewriting a reply to an angry customer does not require the customer’s name, email or IBAN, so leave them out.

What happens to what you paste

  • It is sent to the AI provider's servers and stored in your chat history.
  • Depending on your plan and settings, it may be used to improve models. Business plans usually turn this off; personal accounts often have it on by default.
  • It can be seen by anyone with access to your account, and by the provider under its own policies.

None of this is unusual for a cloud service. The problem is that customer data usually comes with obligations: privacy laws, contracts and your company's own rules.

The real risks

  • Legal: under GDPR and similar laws, personal data shared with a third party needs a legal basis and often a data processing agreement.
  • Contractual: NDAs and customer contracts may forbid sharing their data with outside services.
  • Trust: one screenshot of a chat with a customer's details in it is a bad day for any company.

Get the same help without the data

For most tasks (rewriting a reply, summarising a ticket, drafting a refund email) the AI only needs the situation, not the identity.

Refund for Anna Keller (anna.keller@example.com), order #88231,
IBAN DE89 3704 0044 0532 0130 00, she is upset about the delay.

becomes

Refund for [NAME_1] ([EMAIL_1]), order [ORDER_1],
IBAN [IBAN_1], she is upset about the delay.

The answer is just as good, and you put the real details back before sending it to the customer.
